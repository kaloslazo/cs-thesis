\begin{center}
\large \vspace{-1.5cm} \textbf{Mitigating evolutionary resistance in glioblastoma using adversarial multi-agent reinforcement learning}
\end{center}

This thesis proposes GBMARL, an adversarial multi-agent reinforcement learning framework for studying adaptive temozolomide dosing under evolutionary resistance in glioblastoma. Therapy and tumor agents interact in a simulator of sensitive and resistant populations. MAPPO, with centralized training and decentralized execution, is compared with IPPO, no treatment, maximum dose, and the Gatenby heuristic.

Integration of DepMap Public 26Q1 and GDSC2 recovered 34 cell lines with assays and 24 with complete expression data. Response percentiles across lines represent S/R populations, yielding a relative potency ratio of 9.1. This mapping and the dynamic constants are modeling assumptions; expression does not personalize the policies. Combined time to failure records the first crossing of a burden threshold or resistant majority.

With fifteen seeds per method, MAPPO achieved a median of 34 days [27,41] and IPPO 31 [23,34], exceeding Gatenby's 27 days in 13/15 and 12/15 seeds. The paired comparison was exploratory ($p=0.013759$). EXP-1 showed endpoint dependence: at burden threshold $0.70K$ and resistance threshold $0.50$, MAPPO achieved 17 days versus Gatenby's 27. EXP-2 completed 90 cells and found a MAPPO advantage only in the nominal adversarial regime after Holm correction. The benchmark with five attacker restarts per therapy did not establish lower MAPPO exploitability. These findings are computational evidence conditional on the simulator; health/toxicity remains an extension without clinical calibration.

\noindent \textbf{Keywords:} glioblastoma; adaptive therapy; evolutionary resistance; multi-agent reinforcement learning; MAPPO-CTDE; temozolomide.
